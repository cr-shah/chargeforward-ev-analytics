02 / WHAT SURVIVED CONTACT WITH THE DATA

The interesting result was not “ML wins.”

Useful model work means showing where an approach succeeds, where it fails, and why the aggregate score can hide the local story.

10.3%

Lower pooled RMSE

Poisson GLM reached 92.95 RMSE versus 103.59 for the prior-year baseline across 468 county-month examples.

LOCAL ≠ POOLED
34/39

Counties where RF beat baseline

Random Forest improved most counties, yet a large King County miss made its pooled RMSE worse. The metric and the aggregation level change the conclusion.

75.9%observed

80% interval coverage

Split conformal intervals undercovered by 4.1 points. The site exposes that weakness instead of turning uncertainty into a confidence claim.

MODEL SELECTION RISK
−1.52

Statewide polynomial R²

Earlier validation favored a polynomial trend. It failed in the final historical year, demonstrating why temporal backtests and simple baselines matter.

04 / ENGINEERING JUDGMENT

The choices behind the score.

A model is only as credible as the decisions around its data, evaluation, and failure handling.

01

Time before randomness

All model selection uses expanding or chronological splits. No random train/test split lets future transaction patterns leak backward.

Temporal validity
02

Stock is not flow

The current EV population snapshot is excluded from historical forecast features. Fleet records and registration transactions remain separate measures.

Feature availability
03

Baselines earn their place

A prior-year seasonal model stays in every comparison. It exposed the statewide polynomial failure and kept the county result honest.

Evaluation rigor
04

Missing means unknown

Zero range values are flagged, not interpreted as zero-mile capability. Threshold analysis only uses positive observed ranges and displays coverage.

Data provenance
